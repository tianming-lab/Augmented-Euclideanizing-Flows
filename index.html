Structure of AEF. The conditional normalizing flows model takes the robotic arm's end-effector pose $\mathbf{x}_c$ as input and utilizes the representation $\mathbf{h}$ obtained from feature extraction of environmental images ${{\mathbf{x}}_{nc}}$ as conditions. This setup maps the inputs into a latent variable space. Within this space, a dynamical system is defined by $\mathbf{\dot{z}}=-\mathbf{z}$, where the velocity is modulated by fully connected layers and ensured to be positive through exponential transformation. Ultimately, the desired end-effector velocity output in task space is achieved by computing the inverse of the Jacobian matrix of latent variable $\mathbf{z}$ concerning ${{\mathbf{x}}_{c}}$, mapping the latent space velocity back to task space.

Abstract

Dynamical system-based imitation learning supports formal analysis of system behavior, but often lacks flexibility in multitask settings with high-dimensional observations. To address this, we propose the Augmented Euclideanizing Flows (AEF) framework, which constructs diffeomorphic mappings from complex observations to a shared latent space that incorporates exponentially convergent dynamical systems as a structured prior, providing a robust basis for policy learning. To mitigate the impact of time-varying visual features on latent space consistency, the framework introduces a semantic consistency regularization term during training as a soft constraint, enhancing robustness against visual disturbances and supporting the use of asynchronously collected and unlabeled data. Additionally, by aligning the goal with the latent space origin, implicit target learning is achieved, eliminating the need for explicit detection. Experiments in simulation and real-world settings demonstrate that AEF effectively maintains task performance in disturbed environments.
